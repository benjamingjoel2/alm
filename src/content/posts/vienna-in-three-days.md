Early on Stephansplatz, before the shops along Graben and Kärntner Strasse have rolled up their shutters, the square belongs to delivery vans, pigeons and people cutting across on their way to work. Above them the cathedral's south tower, the one the Viennese call the Steffl, catches the light first while the lanes below are still in shade.

Three days is enough to see Vienna well, as long as you accept that you will not see all of it. Our plan gives each day one big sight, one place to eat or drink slowly and one evening worth dressing up a little for. It works best with a central base, a transport ticket in your pocket and two or three bookings made before you arrive.

## Day 1: the Innere Stadt and the Ringstrasse

Start at St Stephen's Cathedral. The nave is the reason to come, but if your legs are willing, the south tower is the reward: [343 steps up a historic staircase to the old watchmen's room](https://www.stephanskirche.at/visitSouthtower.php?setLang=en), with the roofs of the old town spread out below. The tower is reached only by stairs, and the cathedral's own site lists the daily hours, the last ascent and an all-inclusive ticket that bundles both towers, a catacomb tour and an audio-guided walk around the church.

From Stephansplatz it is a short stroll along Graben and Kohlmarkt to the Hofburg, the former imperial residence that the tourist board describes as [one of the biggest palace complexes in the world](https://www.wien.info/en/art-culture/imperial-sights/hofburg-vienna). Today it holds the Spanish Riding School, the Imperial Treasury, the State Hall of the National Library and a string of museums. Pick one or two rather than trying to do them all.

The classic choice is the [Sisi Museum and the Imperial Apartments](https://www.sisimuseum-hofburg.at/en/), the preserved living quarters of Emperor Franz Joseph and Empress Elisabeth. The Sisi Museum reopens in a redesigned form, with additional rooms, on 19 November 2026. Admission is by fixed time slot, and the official site warns that genuine tickets are sold online only through imperialtickets.com.

By early afternoon you will want to sit down, and this is the part of town to do it. Within a few minutes of the Hofburg, wien.info's list of [coffee houses in the 1st district](https://www.wien.info/en/dine-drink/coffeehouses/in-the-old-city-348958) includes Hawelka on Dorotheergasse, Bräunerhof on Stallburggasse and Demel on Kohlmarkt. One caution: [Café Central closed in March 2026 for a full renovation](https://www.cafecentral.wien/en/), with reopening scheduled for winter 2026, and its team runs a stand-in called DECENTRAL on the Freyung meanwhile. Our [guide to Vienna's coffee houses](/stories/vienna-coffee-houses-guide/) covers what to order and how long you can linger.

In the late afternoon, walk out to the Ringstrasse, the broad boulevard that loops around the old town past Parliament, City Hall and the University. Café Landtmann opened on this stretch in 1873, the year Vienna hosted the World Expo, and it is [one of a handful of survivors of the original 27 cafés](https://www.wien.info/en/dine-drink/coffeehouses/wiener-kaffeehaus-fun-facts-433242) that once lined the Ring.

Finish at the Vienna State Opera. Seats go on sale for the current month and the following two, and performances further out can be pre-ordered, according to the [Staatsoper's ticket information](https://www.wiener-staatsoper.at/en/ticket-info). If you would rather decide on the day, the house's 435 standing places are the Viennese way in. They are sold only on the day of the performance, online from 10 am, at particularly affordable prices, and each place has its own subtitle screen with English, German and the original language always available. Check current fares and the latest rules on the Staatsoper site before you go.

## Day 2: Schönbrunn, the Naschmarkt and a great museum

Take the U4 to [Schönbrunn station](https://www.schoenbrunn.at/en/visitor-information/how-to-get-here), and aim to be there soon after the palace opens. Admission to the palace runs on [fixed entry times](https://www.schoenbrunn.at/en/tickets-and-prices/all-tickets-tours), so book a slot online in advance. The Palace Ticket takes in the whole piano nobile with an audio guide, including the private apartments of Franz Joseph and Elisabeth and Maria Theresa's Rococo chambers; the shorter State Apartments ticket covers a selection of the ceremonial rooms. The palace's own [FAQ](https://www.schoenbrunn.at/en/visitor-information/faq) recommends coming in the morning to avoid long waits.

Leave time for the gardens, which open earlier and close later than the palace itself, according to the [opening times on the palace website](https://www.schoenbrunn.at/en/). Between 6 November 2026 and 6 January 2027 there is also a Christmas market at the palace and a light trail in the park; our round-up of [Austria's Christmas markets](/stories/austria-christmas-markets/) has more on the season.

Ride the U4 back towards the centre and get off at Kettenbrückengasse for lunch at the [Naschmarkt](https://www.wien.info/en/dine-drink/markets/naschmarkt-353536). Vienna's best-known market has around 130 stalls, a newer market hall and plenty of places to eat, from Viennese to Vietnamese. Its listed hours run Monday to Saturday, and on Saturdays a weekly flea market adds to the crowd.

In the afternoon, choose one great collection and give it your full attention. The Kunsthistorisches Museum's [Picture Gallery](https://www.khm.at/en/exhibitions/picture-gallery) holds over 770 works spanning four centuries, including Pieter Bruegel the Elder's Hunters in the Snow. Since 8 September 2026, [admission is by reserved time slot](https://www.khm.at/en/visit), so book ahead; the museum is open daily, with a late opening on Thursdays.

If Klimt matters more to you than Bruegel, go to the Upper Belvedere instead. The Belvedere acquired [The Kiss during Klimt's lifetime](https://www.belvedere.at/en/picture), and it hangs in the collection display alongside work from Cranach to Lassnig. Tram D stops at Schloss Belvedere, and the [Upper Belvedere](https://www.belvedere.at/en/visit/upper-belvedere) is open every day.

## Day 3: the MuseumsQuartier, then up to the vineyards

Spend the morning at the MuseumsQuartier, which the tourist board says began life in 1725 as Fischer von Erlach's [imperial court stables](https://www.wien.info/en/art-culture/museums-exhibitions/top-museums/mq/museumsquartier-wien-880280). The Leopold Museum and mumok both stand in the main courtyard, and the MQ Libelle viewing platform sits on the Leopold's roof. The [grounds themselves are open around the clock and free](https://www.mqw.at/en/the-mq/), with the colourful Enzi seats out in the warmer months and "Winter at the MQ" punch stands in the cold ones.

After lunch, head for the edge of the city, where the Vienna Woods meet the vineyards. The easy way up is bus 38A from Heiligenstadt U-Bahn station, which reaches the [Kahlenberg via Grinzing](https://www.wien.info/en/livable-vienna/parks-green-spaces/kahlenberg-337908) in under half an hour. From the terrace at the top you look across the whole city to the Danube.

If you would rather walk, City Hiking Trail 1 starts at the terminus of tram D in Nussdorf and climbs through vineyards and woods to the Kahlenberg; the [full loop takes about three and a half to four hours](https://www.wien.info/en/livable-vienna/walks-in-vienna/hiking-in-around-347878), and the climb alone is roughly an hour. Trail 1a is a variant along the Danube promenade that ends in Kahlenbergerdorf.

Either way, come down to a Heuriger for the evening. A genuine Viennese wine tavern serves wine from its own vineyards and [hangs out pine branches and an "Ausg'steckt" sign](https://www.wien.info/en/dine-drink/wine/viennese-heurige-347736) when it is open. Many open all year, but smaller ones keep only certain dates, so check the tavern's own website first. Grinzing and Nussdorf are the classic choice; Stammersdorf, across the Danube, is less well known.

## Getting around

Vienna's public transport is the easiest way to link all of this. A ticket for the [Vienna core zone](https://www.wienerlinien.at/web/wl-en/tickets) covers the U-Bahn, trams, buses and ÖBB trains within the city, but not journeys to and from the airport or the City Airport Train. For visitors, Wiener Linien sells single journeys, a 24 Hours Vienna ticket and a 7 Days Vienna ticket that starts on the date you choose. Check current fares on wienerlinien.at; fares change from 1 January 2027.

The [Vienna City Card](https://www.wien.info/en/travel-info/vienna-city-card) is the tourist board's alternative: public transport plus discounts at museums, attractions and some cafés, sold for 24, 48 or 72 hours or seven days. Whether it pays off depends on how many of the discounted places you actually visit, so compare it against a plain transport ticket.

The U-Bahn runs from about 5 am to about midnight, and [around the clock on Friday and Saturday nights](https://www.wien.info/en/travel-info/tourist-info/opening-times-354624) and the eve of public holidays. Night buses cover the main routes on the other nights.

## When to book, and what Sunday looks like

Most of this plan works on the day, but a few things reward planning:

- **Opera seats:** on sale for the current month and the next two; standing places only on the day.
- **Schönbrunn and the Sisi Museum:** fixed entry times, booked online through the official shop.
- **Kunsthistorisches Museum:** time-slot tickets since September 2026.
- **Heurige:** opening dates vary, especially at smaller taverns outside the warm months.

Sundays need a little thought. According to the tourist board's [opening times page](https://www.wien.info/en/travel-info/tourist-info/opening-times-354624), shops usually close on Sundays, with exceptions at the big railway stations, the airport and museum shops. The Naschmarkt's listed hours stop at Saturday, so avoid putting Day 2 on a Sunday. Most museums open daily, though some close one day a week, usually Monday or Tuesday, and some restaurants outside the centre close on Sundays.

## Practical notes

- Buy tickets only from official sites. Schönbrunn, the Sisi Museum and the Kunsthistorisches Museum all warn about fake ticket websites.
- Under-19s go free at the Kunsthistorisches Museum and the Upper Belvedere, according to their admission tables.
- The south tower of St Stephen's is stairs only and not step-free.
- Café Central is closed for renovation in 2026; check its website for the reopening date.
- Bus 38A to the Kahlenberg runs several times an hour, and more often at weekends and on public holidays.
