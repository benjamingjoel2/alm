Early on a summer morning, before the tour coaches arrive, the old town of Salzburg belongs to delivery vans, church bells and the people who live here. The squares around the cathedral are swept and quiet, the fortress catches the first sun on its ridge, and the Salzach slides past, green and fast, towards the north.

Most first-time visitors arrive with a soundtrack already playing in their heads. The film made Salzburg famous around the world, and there is no harm in humming along. But the city was a seat of archbishops, a cradle of Mozart and one of Europe's great festival towns long before Hollywood found it, and it rewards anyone who gives it a little more time than a coach stop allows.

## The old town the archbishops built

Salzburg's Old Town was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996, according to the [Austrian National Tourist Office](https://www.austria.info/en/highlights/unesco-world-heritage-sites). The [Austrian Commission for UNESCO](https://www.unesco.at/en/culture/world-heritage/world-heritage-sites-in-austria) sums it up neatly: "The old city centre of Salzburg is an outstanding example of a seat of ecclesiastical power located at the heart of Europe."

That is the key to reading the place. The cathedral, the Residenz and the run of squares between them are the stage set of that ecclesiastical power, and the fortress above them was begun by an archbishop. Music is part of the listing too: the city's own [UNESCO questions-and-answers page](https://www.salzburg.info/en/magazin/city-feeling/unesco-world-heritage-salzburg-10-questions-and-answers_a_40167596) notes that the citation links Salzburg with music "in the person of its famous son, Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart."

Our advice is simple: walk it before you sightsee it. Wander the lanes and passageways off the main streets early or late in the day, when the light is low and the crowds are thin, and save the interiors for the middle hours.

## Up to Hohensalzburg

The fortress on the Festungsberg is the first thing you see from almost anywhere in town. [Tourismus Salzburg](https://www.salzburg.info/en/travel-info/infos/hohensalzburg-fortress_az_7955) dates the start of construction to 1077 under Archbishop Gebhard and bills it as the biggest fully preserved castle in Central Europe. Inside are the Fortress Museum, a puppet museum, the historic armoury, the Gothic prince's rooms and a panorama tour up to the keep's observation platform and along the battlements.

There are two ways up. The FestungsBahn funicular has been carrying visitors to the fortress since 1892, and [salzburg.info calls it](https://www.salzburg.info/en/travel-info/arrival-traffic/cable_railway) Austria's oldest funicular still in operation. It closes periodically for maintenance, including a longer stretch in early 2027, when the fortress stays open but is reachable only on foot.

Walking up is no hardship, and the footpath has its own tickets. Go up in the late afternoon in summer, when the fortress keeps longer opening hours, and you get the old town laid out below you in warm light.

## Mozart, in two houses

Mozart's two Salzburg homes are run as museums by the International Mozarteum Foundation, and they tell different parts of the story.

- **Mozart's Birthplace** at Getreidegasse 9 is where Wolfgang Amadé Mozart was born on 27 January 1756. The [Mozarteum's page](https://mozarteum.at/en/museums/mozarts-birthplace) describes a three-storey exhibition on his childhood, family, friends and patrons, with instruments including his childhood violin and his clavichord. A visit takes about an hour.
- **The Mozart Residence** on Makartplatz 8, across the river, gave the family an eight-room apartment from 1773, and Mozart lived there until he left Salzburg for good at the end of 1780. The [Mozarteum notes](https://www.mozarteum.at/en/museums/mozarts-residence) that the house was partly destroyed in the Second World War, rebuilt and reopened to the public in 1996. It also runs changing special exhibitions.

Tickets are sold online or at the museum box offices, and a combined ticket covers both houses. The birthplace is the busier of the two, so it is worth going first thing.

## Festival summer: how tickets actually work

The Salzburg Festival is the city's other great draw. The [festival's own site](https://www.salzburgerfestspiele.at/en) lists the summer 2027 season as 16 July to 29 August, with a shorter Whitsun festival in May, and a programme of opera, drama and concerts that includes the long-running "Jedermann".

Getting tickets is less mysterious than it sounds, but it rewards planning. According to the festival's [ticketing information](https://www.salzburgerfestspiele.at/en/ticketing-information):

- Summer tickets can be ordered once the programme is published, which the festival expects in early December. "Orders must be made in writing", through the website or an order form.
- Orders are collected until a deadline announced in the programme, and "the time of the order has no influence on the processing". Members, patrons and subscribers get priority, and "orders are no guarantee of a ticket allocation."
- When ordering, you choose a sector and price category, not specific seats.
- After the order phase, remaining tickets go on general sale online, by phone and at the ticket office, and at that point you can pick seats.
- "Tickets must be personalized without exception", and the named visitor needs photo ID. E-tickets are delivered free by email.
- Returns are only possible for sold-out performances, resold on commission, and resale is not guaranteed.

If you miss the order window, keep an eye on the direct sale and on returned tickets for sold-out performances.

## Two city mountains: Kapuzinerberg and Mönchsberg

Salzburg's old town is pinned between two forested hills, and each makes a fine half-day walk without leaving the city.

The Kapuzinerberg rises on the right bank. The [city's own walk description](https://www.salzburg.info/en/magazin/city-feeling/city-hike-up-the-kapuzinerberg-mountain_a_9014084) starts through the historic gate on Linzer Gasse and climbs past eleven Calvary chapels to the Capuchin monastery. Just beyond the Felixpforte, the Hettwer Bastion has benches and one of the best views over the town. The path then runs through forest to the Franziskischlössl, built in 1629. The first stretch is steep, and the tourist board recommends shoes with non-slip soles.

The Mönchsberg, on the left bank, is gentler. [Tourismus Salzburg](https://www.salzburg.info/en/magazin/city-feeling/a-mountain-escape-close-at-the-monchsberg_a_273508) describes a route from near the Mülln church past the Museum der Moderne, with its lookout over the city, to the Richterhöhe, the hill's highest point at 508 metres, before dropping down through Nonntal below the fortress.

If your legs need a break, the [Mönchsberg Lift](https://salzburg.info/en/travel-info/arrival-traffic/moenchsberg-lift) from Anton-Neumayr-Platz carries you up to the museum in about 30 seconds. Combine the two hills with the fortress and you have a full day of views.

## Advent in Salzburg

Salzburg in December is a different city, and we think one of its best. The [tourist board's Advent page](https://www.salzburg.info/en/salzburg/advent) describes the main Christkindlmarkt on the squares next to the cathedral and the Residenz, with Advent beginning towards the end of November.

There are smaller markets too, including on Mirabellplatz and up at the fortress, and the Advent market at Hellbrunn is especially popular with families. Look out for the Salzburg Advent Singing, a reflective concert tradition, and for Krampus and Perchten parades, folk customs that run through the season into early January. Joseph Mohr, co-author of "Silent Night", was born in Salzburg.

For the wider picture, from Vienna's squares to small mountain villages, read our guide to [Austria's Christmas markets](/stories/austria-christmas-markets/).

## Out of town: Hellbrunn, the Untersberg and the lakes

A short way south of the centre, Hellbrunn Palace is the archbishops' sense of humour made permanent. The [Hellbrunn site](https://www.hellbrunn.at/en/the-trick-fountains/) explains that Archbishop Markus Sittikus "had a complex built to amaze, entertain, and amuse his guests", with water machines dating from 1615.

The fountains spout from unlikely places, so expect to be splashed. The Mechanical Theatre, added in 1750, has 163 water-powered figures acting out a small Baroque town, with a water-powered organ setting the pace.

The trick fountains run from spring to autumn and rest over the winter, so check the current opening dates on hellbrunn.at before you plan around them. Allow a half day and pair it with a stroll in the palace park.

The Untersberg is the big mountain on the city's southern skyline. Its cable car leaves from Grödig/St. Leonhard, about 10 kilometres south of the city, and [Tourismus Salzburg](https://salzburg.info/en/sights/excursions/cable-car-untersberg) gives the ride as about eight and a half minutes to the top station at 1,776 metres. On a clear day you can see the Salzkammergut lakes and the Chiemsee. City buses 25 and 28 and trolleybus 5 serve the valley station. It closes for maintenance in late autumn and in spring, so check the dates first.

The lakes themselves are the other classic escape. The [Hallstatt tourist board](https://www.hallstatt.net/service-eng/anreise-en-US/) notes that bus line 150 runs from Salzburg into the Salzkammergut, which makes the nearer lakes an easy day out. To do Hallstatt and the Gosau lakes justice, though, we would stay a night or two; our guide to [Hallstatt and the Salzkammergut lakes](/stories/hallstatt-and-the-salzkammergut-lakes/) has the details.

## Practical notes

- **The Salzburg Card.** The [Salzburg Card](https://www.salzburg.info/en/hotels-offers/salzburg-card) comes in 24, 48 and 72-hour versions and gives one-time free admission to the city's attractions and museums, free public transport (the S-Bahn excepted) and discounts elsewhere. A digital version works on your phone straight away. It pays off if you plan two or three paid sights a day; check current prices on the official site.
- **What it covers.** The FestungsBahn, the Mönchsberg Lift and an Untersberg return ride are all included once each. For the Untersberg, the card must still be valid when you come down.
- **Festival tickets.** Order in writing during the order phase if you can, and keep photo ID with you, since tickets are personalised.
- **Timing.** Festival summer and Advent are the busiest periods. Book accommodation early, and plan the most popular interiors for opening time.
- **Shoes.** Both city mountains have steep, sometimes slippery sections. Wear proper footwear, especially after rain.

Salzburg's old town is compact, which is exactly why it repays slowing down: an early fortress, a late walk on the Kapuzinerberg, an evening at a festival concert.
