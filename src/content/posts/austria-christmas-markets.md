Late afternoon on Vienna's Rathausplatz, the light goes early. The neo-Gothic city hall is lit up behind rows of wooden huts, the trees in the park are hung with lights, and the air smells of punch, roasted almonds and woodsmoke. People stand in small knots around high tables with steaming mugs, gloved hands wrapped round them, in no hurry to be anywhere else.

Austria does Advent seriously. The markets are only part of it, but they are the easiest way in for a visitor, and the good news is that the main ones are in cities linked by rail. This is our guide to the main markets, the customs you will meet, and how to fit several into one trip.

## Advent, the Austrian way

A few traditions help make sense of what you see. The [Austrian National Tourist Office](https://www.austria.info/en/things-to-do/skiing-and-winter/christmas-traditions) explains that a candle on the Advent wreath is lit on each of the four Sundays before Christmas, and you will see wreaths everywhere from church doors to café tables. Presents arrive on Christmas Eve, brought not by Father Christmas but by the Christkind, the Christ Child, which is why so many markets are called Christkindlmärkte.

Then there are the darker figures. Around 5 and 6 December, Krampus, a horned and fur-clad creature, [accompanies St Nicholas](https://www.austria.info/en/things-to-do/skiing-and-winter/christmas-traditions), who has his own day on 6 December. Later, during the Rauhnächte, a run of midwinter nights, Perchten roam the streets in masks, ringing bells to drive away winter. Some city markets bring these customs to town: Salzburg's [Christkindlmarkt programme](https://www.salzburg.info/en/events/events-calendar/salzburg-christkindlmarket-on-dom-and-residenzplatz-square_event_42164337) lists a Krampus run on 5 December and a Perchten run on 21 December, which the programme marks as the first Rauhnacht. They are loud, theatrical and occasionally alarming for small children.

## What to eat and drink

The smell of [hot punch](https://www.austria.info/en/things-to-do/skiing-and-winter/christmas-traditions) is the signature of an Austrian market. You will usually have a choice of Glühwein (mulled wine) and Punsch (punch). At Vienna's Rathausplatz, the [stall list](https://christkindlmarkt.at/en/the-market/) includes punch and mulled wine stands as well as one serving only non-alcoholic punch, which is handy if you are travelling with children. In Innsbruck, the [tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en-us/highlights/christmas-markets-in-innsbruck/) describes Glühwein served in a mug alongside Kiachln, a local speciality.

Beyond that, follow your nose:

- **Roasted chestnuts**, whose scent the [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en/inspiration/christmas-markets) singles out at Innsbruck's old town market.
- **Lebkuchen**, Austrian gingerbread, along with home-baked biscuits such as Vanillekipferl and Linzer Augen, recipes the tourist office says are [passed down through generations](https://www.austria.info/en/things-to-do/skiing-and-winter/christmas-traditions). For gingerbread at its most atmospheric, the tourist office points to the basilica town of Mariazell in Styria, where the scent of gingerbread and beeswax fills the lanes around the basilica in Advent.

## Vienna: palaces, lanes and the big one

The [Vienna Tourist Board](https://www.wien.info/en/shopping-wining-dining/markets/christmas-markets) describes the city's squares turning into Christmas markets from mid-November until Christmas. These are the ones we would plan around, with 2026 dates as published by the tourist board and the market operators.

- **Rathausplatz (Wiener Christkindlmarkt):** the big one, open [13 November to 26 December 2026](https://www.christkindlmarkt.at/en/), daily from 10 am to 10 pm, closing at 6.30 pm on 24 December. It has been around for at least 180 years: the [market's own history](https://christkindlmarkt.at/en/the-market/) traces it from the Freyung in 1772 to Am Hof in 1842, with the huts arriving on Rathausplatz in 1975. Since 1959 a different Austrian province, or South Tyrol, has donated the Christmas tree. The surrounding park has the much-photographed heart tree, a giant Advent wreath and the Ice Dream skating rink, which runs until 6 January 2027.
- **Schönbrunn Palace:** an imperial backdrop, traditional crafts and an ice rink, open [6 November 2026 to 6 January 2027](https://www.wien.info/en/shopping-wining-dining/markets/christmas-markets).
- **Spittelberg:** stalls in the narrow lanes of the Biedermeier quarter, with a focus on local, fair-trade and organic goods. Open 13 November to 23 December, from the afternoon on weekdays.
- **Am Hof:** quality handicrafts in the city centre, with Christmas music and choral singing. Open 13 November to 23 December.
- **Belvedere Palace:** a Baroque setting in the palace grounds and a market inspired by Austria's imperial past. Open 20 November to 31 December.

The [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en/inspiration/christmas-markets) also lists a market on Stephansplatz, beside the cathedral, from 6 November to 26 December. Opening hours differ widely between these markets, so check the [tourist board's list](https://www.wien.info/en/shopping-wining-dining/markets/christmas-markets) before you go. For the rest of your time in the city, see [Vienna in three days](/stories/vienna-in-three-days/).

## Salzburg: the old town and the palace

Salzburg's [Christkindlmarkt on Domplatz and Residenzplatz](https://www.salzburg.info/en/events/events-calendar/salzburg-christkindlmarket-on-dom-and-residenzplatz-square_event_42164337) fills the squares around the cathedral in the UNESCO-listed old town. In 2026 it runs from 19 November to 1 January 2027. On 24 December it closes at 3 pm, and on New Year's Eve the food and drink stands stay open until 1 am. Alongside the stalls, the programme includes choir performances, traditional tower blowing by brass players, the Krampus and Perchten runs, and a nativity exhibition in the Residenzhof.

Away from the old town, [Hellbrunn Advent Magic](https://www.hellbrunneradventzauber.at/) runs from 19 November to 24 December 2026 at Hellbrunn Palace. The palace facade becomes a giant Advent calendar with 24 windows, and over 700 fir trees hung with red baubles fill the grounds. There is a children's area too, including a post office for letters to the Christkind. Higher up, the [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en/inspiration/christmas-markets) lists an Advent market in the courtyard of Hohensalzburg Fortress from 27 November to 20 December, open Friday to Sunday plus 7 and 8 December. If you have more days in the city, our guide to [Salzburg beyond The Sound of Music](/stories/salzburg-beyond-the-sound-of-music/) has ideas for the daylight hours.

### A detour to Oberndorf

"Silent Night" was first sung on [24 December 1818](https://www.salzburgerland.com/en/silent-night/) in St Nikola church in Oberndorf near Salzburg. Joseph Mohr, a priest, wrote the words, and Franz Xaver Gruber, a teacher, composed the melody at his request. Today a Silent Night Chapel and museum in Oberndorf [commemorate that first performance](https://www.austria.info/en/things-to-do/skiing-and-winter/christmas-traditions).

In Advent there is a small [Silent Night Advent Market](https://www.salzburger-seenland.at/en/lake-district-highlights/silent-night-advent-market) on Silent Night Square, open Thursday to Sunday from 20 November to 24 December 2026, with a few extra days. Every Christmas Eve from 4.45 pm, people gather in front of the Silent Night Memorial Chapel for a service honouring Gruber and Mohr. It is a quiet, moving contrast to the city squares.

## Innsbruck: under the Golden Roof

Innsbruck's [Old Town Christkindlmarkt](https://www.austria.info/en-us/highlights/christmas-markets-in-innsbruck/) sits in front of the Golden Roof in the historic centre and runs from 13 November to 23 December 2026, daily from 11 am to 9 pm. The [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en/inspiration/christmas-markets) mentions tower trumpeters playing at the Golden Roof, and the mountains rise straight up behind the rooftops.

The city has several other markets, each with its own character. Maria-Theresien-Straße has a more modern feel and stays open until 6 January 2027. The Hungerburg market, reached by funicular, adds views over the city, while the Marktplatz market is aimed at families, with a carousel and petting zoo.

## A quieter market by the lake

If the city markets feel crowded, head to the Salzkammergut. The [Wolfgangsee Advent](https://www.wolfgangseer-advent.at/en/) spreads across three lakeside villages, St. Gilgen, St. Wolfgang and Strobl, from 20 November to 20 December 2026. It opens Wednesday to Sunday, plus 7 and 8 December, and entry is free.

Each village does something different. St. Wolfgang has a nativity scene of more than 70 life-size figures around the village, Strobl turns itself into a nativity village, and St. Gilgen has St Nicholas scenes on the church square. The symbol of it all is a 16-metre floating peace lantern on the lake in front of St. Wolfgang, and the lake's boat service links the three markets. Buses run from Salzburg to St. Gilgen and Strobl.

## Before you go

- **Dates:** most markets open in the second half of November, says the [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en/inspiration/christmas-markets). City markets often run daily until 24 December, some until New Year's Eve or 6 January, while rural markets may open only on weekends. Check the official site before you travel.
- **Christmas Eve:** many markets close early on 24 December or end on 23 December. Plan your last market visit accordingly.
- **Layers:** you will be standing outdoors for hours. Warm boots, a hat and gloves matter more than a heavy coat.
- **Trains:** Vienna, Salzburg and Innsbruck are linked by rail, so you can see three cities' markets without a car.
