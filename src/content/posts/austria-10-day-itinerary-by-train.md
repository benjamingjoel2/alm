On a weekday morning at a big Austrian station, the rhythm is unhurried. The departure board flips through the western cities, people buy a coffee and a pastry for the journey, and luggage is lifted into the racks. Soon after departure the suburbs have thinned into fields, and by the time the coffee is finished the hills have begun.

That is the case for seeing Austria by train. The country's main sights sit along one railway, the trains are frequent, and you never have to think about parking in an old town. This ten-day route follows that line from Vienna to Innsbruck, with the Danube and the lakes along the way.

## How the route works

The spine of this trip is the Western Line. ÖBB says trains run between [Vienna and Salzburg twice an hour](https://www.oebb.at/en/reiseplanung-services/oebb-zugverbindungen/zugverbindungen-oesterreich), and that Railjet Xpress services run hourly from Vienna via St. Pölten, Linz, Salzburg and Innsbruck to Feldkirch. Every move in this itinerary is on or just off that line.

Here is the shape of it, nine nights in all:

- **Days 1–3: Vienna** (3 nights)
- **Day 4: The Wachau** (1 night in Melk or Krems, or a day trip from Vienna with a fourth night there)
- **Days 5–7: Salzburg**, with a day in Hallstatt (3 nights)
- **Days 8–10: Innsbruck** (2 nights, leaving on day 10)

If you prefer fewer moves, take the Wachau as a day trip and drop a night in Salzburg. If you prefer more mountains, take a night from Vienna and give it to Innsbruck.

## Days 1–3: Vienna

Three nights is the minimum we would give Vienna on a first visit. Its historic centre is a World Heritage site that Austria's tourist office describes as reflecting [three centuries of European urban development](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/highlights/unesco-world-heritage-sites/), from Gothic and Baroque to the Ringstrasse era, and it is dense enough that you will want to walk it slowly.

We have written a full plan for these days in [Vienna in three days](/stories/vienna-in-three-days/), and a guide to the city's [coffee houses](/stories/vienna-coffee-houses-guide/). The short version for this itinerary:

- **Day 1:** Arrive, drop bags and walk the old centre. Keep the afternoon light.
- **Day 2:** One big museum or palace in the morning, a coffee house in the afternoon.
- **Day 3:** A slower day for a neighbourhood, a market or a wine tavern.

Use these days to set up the rest of the trip. Download the ÖBB app, book the longer journeys and reserve seats (more on that below).

## Day 4: The Wachau

The Wachau is the stretch of the Danube that the tourist office describes as [36 kilometres of vineyard terraces, villages, monasteries, castles and ruins](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/highlights/unesco-world-heritage-sites/) in Lower Austria. It is the gentlest possible introduction to the countryside, and it sits neatly between Vienna and the west.

The classic way to see it uses train and boat:

1. **Train to Melk.** Melk Abbey says there are [regular train connections to Melk from Vienna and St. Pölten](https://www.stiftmelk.at/en/visit-experience/translate-to-english-anreise-zum-stift-melk/), and that the abbey is roughly a 10–15 minute walk from the station through the town centre.
2. **Visit the abbey.** Check the [abbey's opening hours](https://www.stiftmelk.at/en/visit-experience/opening-hours-prices-guided-tours/) first: the longest daily hours run from April to October, and for part of the winter visits are by guided tour only.
3. **Boat downstream.** The Danube tourist board says several shipping companies run [regular services, most of them between Krems and Melk](https://www.donau.com/en/wachau-nibelungengau-kremstal/shipping-ferries). Sailings are seasonal, so check the operators' timetables for your dates.
4. **Train back or onward from Krems.** Krems station is the [terminus of the ÖBB line from Vienna's Franz-Josefs-Bahnhof](https://www.donau.com/en/infrastructure/krems-on-the-danube-train-station), with a connecting line to St. Pölten.

As a day trip from Vienna, that loop brings you home in the evening. If you stay the night in Melk or Krems instead, you can rejoin the Railjet line at St. Pölten the next morning and carry on west to Salzburg. For a slower version with bikes, see our [Wachau by bike and boat](/stories/wachau-valley-by-bike-and-boat/) guide.

## Days 5–7: Salzburg and Hallstatt

Salzburg's old town has been [a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1996](https://www.salzburg.info/en/salzburg/unesco-world-cultural-heritage). The city's tourist board says the protected area covers about 236 hectares on both banks of the Salzach, with the fortress, the cathedral, Getreidegasse and the Mirabell Gardens all inside it. Give it two full days on foot; our guide to [Salzburg beyond The Sound of Music](/stories/salzburg-beyond-the-sound-of-music/) has ideas.

Use the middle day for Hallstatt. This is the most complicated journey on the trip, so it helps to understand it before you go:

- **By train.** Hallstatt's tourist board explains that the [Salzkammergut Railway runs from Attnang-Puchheim](https://www.hallstatt.net/service-eng/anreise-en-US/austria-by-rail/), a station on the Western Line between Vienna and Salzburg. From Salzburg you take a train east to Attnang-Puchheim and change there.
- **The station is across the lake.** Hallstatt station is on the opposite shore from the village. The ferry pier is a two-minute walk below the station, boats run almost hourly, and the crossing takes about 15 minutes.
- **By bus, as an alternative.** The tourist board's [bus page](https://hallstatt.net/service-eng/anreise-en-US/austria-by-bus/) describes line 150 from Salzburg's main station past Lake Fuschl and Lake Wolfgang to Bad Ischl, where you continue towards Hallstatt by bus or train. It notes that buses from Bad Ischl towards Hallstatt stop in the early evening.

Whichever way you go, check the last ferry and the last train back before you set out, and leave the return open rather than squeezing in one more stop. For what to do once you are there, read our guide to [Hallstatt and the Salzkammergut lakes](/stories/hallstatt-and-the-salzkammergut-lakes/).

## Days 8–10: Innsbruck

The Railjet west from Salzburg brings you to Innsbruck, the one city on this route where the mountains begin at the edge of town. The Nordkette cable cars say you can get [from the old town to the Hungerburg in just eight minutes](https://nordkette.com/en/) on the funicular, then continue by cable car to the Seegrube and up to the Hafelekar.

Plan the Nordkette for the clearest of your Innsbruck days and keep the other for the old town. If you want to walk once you are up high, read the mountain safety section of our [first-timer's guide](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-austria/) first. In winter, our guide to [skiing in Tyrol for first-timers](/stories/skiing-in-tyrol-for-first-timers/) picks up from here.

On day 10, use the morning for one last walk, then travel on or home.

## Booking, seats and luggage

You can do almost everything through ÖBB:

- **The app.** The [ÖBB app](https://www.oebb.at/en/tickets-kundenkarten/online-mobile-ticketing/oebb-app) sells tickets, shows platform and train number, and lets you choose a Railjet seat from a seat map.
- **Sparschiene fares.** [Sparschiene](https://www.oebb.at/en/tickets-kundenkarten/oesterreich-europa/sparschiene) is ÖBB's limited, train-specific cheaper fare, bookable up to six months ahead. Each ticket is valid only on the train you chose, and the basic version cannot be refunded. The Komfort version includes a seat reservation and allows cancellation. Fares vary by date and train, so compare in the app.
- **Seat reservations.** ÖBB [recommends reserving](https://www.oebb.at/en/reiseplanung-services/vor-ihrer-reise/reservierung-sitzplatz) if you want to be sure of travelling on your first-choice train, and notes that demand can exceed capacity on busy days. Weekends and holidays are when we would always reserve.
- **KlimaTicket.** The [KlimaTicket Ö](https://www.klimaticket.at/en/) is an annual pass for scheduled public transport across Austria. It is built for residents rather than a ten-day visit, and it does not cover tourist railways such as the Wachaubahn.

Luggage is easier than it looks. ÖBB lists [lockers at Wien Hauptbahnhof, Wien Mitte, Salzburg Hauptbahnhof and Innsbruck Hauptbahnhof](https://www.oebb.at/en/reiseplanung-services/am-bahnhof/gepaeckaufbewahrung), which you can book on site or in advance. That makes a stopover between check-out and check-in simple. For the Hallstatt day, leave your bags at the hotel in Salzburg.

If you would rather not carry cases at all, ÖBB's [door-to-door luggage service](https://www.oebb.at/en/reiseplanung-services/vor-ihrer-reise/haus-haus-gepaeck) collects bags from one address in Austria and delivers them to another, usually the next working day. It must be booked with a valid ÖBB ticket, by phone or at a ticket counter, at least three working days before collection, and there are no collections or deliveries on Sundays or public holidays.

## Extending to Carinthia or Vorarlberg

With a few more days, the route stretches easily in two directions.

- **West to Vorarlberg.** The same Railjet Xpress line continues from Innsbruck to Feldkirch, and ÖBB says direct RJX services also run from Bregenz every two hours. It is a natural add-on at the far western end of the country.
- **South to Carinthia.** Carinthia is on a separate route from Vienna, with ÖBB listing 26 daily services from Vienna to Klagenfurt. Its tourist board calls it [the land of lakes](https://www.visitcarinthia.at/activities/summer/lakes-experience/carinthia-the-land-of-lakes), with Wörthersee described as one of the warmest lakes in the Alps. It works best as a summer extension, either before Vienna or as a separate loop.

## Practical notes

- Book Railjet journeys early if you want Sparschiene fares, and remember they are tied to one train.
- Reserve seats for weekends, holidays and any journey with luggage.
- Download the ÖBB app before you leave home and use it for platforms and changes.
- Keep the Hallstatt day flexible and confirm the last ferry and train back.
- Check boat and abbey seasons in the Wachau before fixing dates.
- Use station lockers or the door-to-door service rather than dragging cases through old towns.

Ten days is enough to see the country change from river to lake to mountain without ever feeling rushed, as long as you let the trains do the work. If you want help deciding how many nights to spend where, Danube & Alps can suggest a route at a slow, medium or full tempo.
