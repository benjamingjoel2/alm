The first boat of the morning noses away from the landing at Krems, and within minutes the town gives way to vineyards. Dry stone terraces climb the slopes on both banks, a blue church tower comes into view at Dürnstein, and along the riverside path cyclists keep pace with the boat for a while before the current wins. This is the Wachau: a short, slow stretch of the Danube that rewards anyone who travels it at river speed.

It is also one of the easiest escapes from Vienna. You can see it in a long day, but it is better over two or three, with time to ride, sail, climb to a castle ruin and sit in a wine tavern while the light goes.

## What the Wachau is

The Wachau is the stretch of the Danube valley between Melk and Krems in Lower Austria. The [Austrian National Tourist Office](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/regions/wachau-valley/) notes it was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000, and the [site's own management team](https://www.weltkulturerbe-wachau.at/en/detailed-view/rwd_projects/20-years-of-the-wachau-world-heritage-site) sums up what makes it distinctive: dry stone walls, terraced vineyards, apricot orchards, dry grasslands and a free-flowing Danube.

The main places strung along the river are Melk, Spitz an der Donau, Weißenkirchen, Dürnstein and Krems. None of them is large, and the valley is narrow enough that you are rarely out of sight of a vineyard, a church spire or a ruined castle on a crag.

## Getting there from Vienna

The Wachau has a station at each end, which makes it ideal for a one-way trip by bike or boat.

- **Melk (western end):** trains from Vienna stop at Melk station, and [Lower Austria's tourist board](https://lower-austria.info/excursions/stift-melk) puts the abbey about ten minutes' walk from the station.
- **Krems (eastern end):** Krems station is the terminus of the [ÖBB line from Vienna's Franz-Josefs-Bahnhof](https://lower-austria.info/infrastructure/a-krems-on-the-danube-train-station).

The [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/regions/wachau-valley/) describes the valley as about an hour by train from Vienna. Check current times and fares on [oebb.at](https://www.oebb.at/en/reiseplanung-services/im-zug/fahrradmitnahme) before you go, as some journeys involve a change. If you are building the Wachau into a longer rail trip, our [ten-day itinerary by train](/stories/austria-10-day-itinerary-by-train/) shows how it fits between Vienna and Salzburg, and [Vienna in three days](/stories/vienna-in-three-days/) covers the city you will most likely be coming from.

## Riding the Danube Cycle Path

The Danube Cycle Path (Donauradweg) runs right through the Wachau, and this is one of its best-loved sections. The official north-bank stage from [Emmersdorf to Krems](https://www.donau.com/en/tour/danube-cycle-path-stage-5-north-bank-emmersdorf-krems) is a little over 34 km, rated moderate, and almost flat. It passes through Aggsbach Markt, Willendorf, Spitz, Wösendorf, Weißenkirchen and Dürnstein before reaching Krems.

Things to look out for along that stage, as listed by the regional tourist board:

- The view across to the Aggstein castle ruins near Aggsbach.
- Willendorf, where the famous Venus figurine was found (it is now in Vienna's Natural History Museum).
- The fortified church of St Michael in Weißenkirchen.
- Dürnstein's abbey and castle ruins.
- The Steiner Tor, the old gate that is Krems's symbol.

Be aware that for much of the way the route follows the B3 road, according to the [stage description](https://www.donau.com/en/tour/danube-cycle-path-stage-5-north-bank-emmersdorf-krems), so it is not a traffic-free trail throughout. The page also posts roadworks and closure notices, so check it shortly before you ride. If you want to swap banks, the regional board lists [cable ferries](https://www.donau.com/en/ferries) at Spitz–Arnsdorf and Weißenkirchen–St. Lorenz and a ferry at Dürnstein–Rossatz; check each one's hours, as low water can affect services.

### Bikes: bring or hire?

ÖBB is candid about this. On regional trains, bike spaces are limited and [cannot be reserved](https://www.oebb.at/en/reiseplanung-services/im-zug/fahrradmitnahme), and you need a bicycle ticket as well as your own. Long-distance trains need a bike ticket and a reservation. ÖBB names the Danube Cycle Path through the Wachau as a popular route where spaces can run out, and suggests booking early or hiring a bike at your destination.

Hiring locally is simple. Wachau Explorer in Krems, for example, rents trekking bikes, e-bikes and mountain e-bikes, and the [regional tourist board's listing](https://www.donau.com/en/infrastructure/wachau-explorer-touren-fahrradverleih) says it can deliver bikes to hotels and meeting points around Krems, the Wachau and Melk. One more thing to know: the same [stage page](https://www.donau.com/en/tour/danube-cycle-path-stage-5-north-bank-emmersdorf-krems) notes that bicycles are not allowed on the local buses at Emmersdorf.

## Taking to the river

The classic Wachau day pairs a ride one way with a boat the other. DDSG Blue Danube runs scheduled sailings between Krems and Melk, calling at Dürnstein, Weißenkirchen, Spitz and Emmersdorf, though not every boat stops everywhere. On the [2027 timetable](https://ddsg-blue-danube.at/en/wachau/), the Krems-to-Melk sailings take roughly three hours.

On bikes, DDSG is clear: [bicycles can be taken on all cruises](https://ddsg-blue-danube.at/en/wachau/), but spaces are limited and bike tickets are sold only on site, not through the online shop. Groups should ask ahead.

The season matters. The [regional tourist board](https://www.donau.com/en/excursions/ddsg-blue-danube) listed the 2026 scheduled service as running daily from 28 March to 1 November, so by the time you read this the boats are laid up for winter. DDSG has already published [2027 dates](https://ddsg-blue-danube.at/en/wachau/): a pre-season from 20 March to 23 April, the main season from 24 April to 3 October, and a post-season from 4 October to 1 November. DDSG also flags that low water can change the schedule, so check its site on the day.

## Melk, Dürnstein and the villages between

**Melk Abbey** dominates the western end of the valley. Benedictine monks have lived and worked there [since 1089](https://www.stiftmelk.at/en/), and the abbey describes itself as one of the biggest Baroque ensembles in Europe. Visits take in the abbey church, the library, the museum in the former imperial rooms and the abbey park. Opening hours change with the seasons: from 7 January to 19 March 2027, weekday visits are by [guided tour only](https://www.stiftmelk.at/en/visit-experience/opening-hours-prices-guided-tours/), with English tours at set times, while from April to October the abbey is open all day with or without a guide.

**Dürnstein** is the postcard. Above the town sit the ruins of the castle of the Kuenringer dynasty, where, the [regional tourist board](https://www.donau.com/en/municipality/duernstein) notes, the English king Richard the Lionheart was held prisoner from 1192 to 1193. The ruins are freely accessible all year and the climb is rewarded with long views over the valley. Down by the water, the abbey's striking blue church tower is the image most people carry home from the Wachau.

**Spitz** and **Weißenkirchen** are wine villages first. The [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/regions/wachau-valley/) singles out Spitz's steep Tausendeimerberg vineyard and calls Weißenkirchen the largest wine-growing community in the Wachau. Both are natural places to break a ride for lunch.

## Apricots, wine and Heurigen

Two things grow on these slopes that the Wachau is known for. The first is the apricot. The **Wachauer Marille** is [protected as an EU designation of origin](https://www.weltkulturerbe-wachau.at/en/wine-tourism), sold by member farms under a shared seal, and the fruit goes into jams, dumplings, brandies and liqueurs. Austrian-grown apricots are in season in [July and August](https://www.gesundheit.gv.at/leben/ernaehrung/saisonkalender/juli/marillen).

The second is wine. The valley is best known for [Riesling and Grüner Veltliner](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/regions/wachau-valley/), grown on terraces held up by those dry stone walls; the [World Heritage team](https://www.weltkulturerbe-wachau.at/en/wine-tourism) notes that viticulture here goes back to Roman times.

The best place to drink it is where it is made. A Heuriger or Buschenschank is a winemaker's own tavern. As the [Vienna Tourist Board](https://www.wien.info/en/viennese-wine-heurige-347736) explains for its own wine villages, you recognise a genuine one by the pine bushes hung outside and a sign reading "Ausg'steckt", which also tells you when it is open. In the Wachau, the [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/de-at/highlights/heurige-und-buschenschaenken-wachau/) draws the distinction clearly: a Buschenschank serves only its own wine with cold food, while a Heuriger may open year-round and serve hot dishes too. Many open only for set periods, so check the [online Heurigen calendar](https://www.donau.com/en/online-heurigen-calendar) before you set off.

## When to go

- **Late March to early April:** apricot blossom, which the [national tourist office](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/regions/wachau-valley/) dates to the end of March and beginning of April. The boats return around the same time.
- **May:** Wachau Wine Spring begins at the start of the month, according to the same page.
- **July and August:** apricot season, and the main boat season is in full swing.
- **Autumn:** harvest-time colour on the terraces, with boats running into early November.
- **Winter:** quiet, with no scheduled boats and shorter abbey hours, but the valley is still an easy train trip from Vienna.

## Before you go

- Check ÖBB for trains to Melk and Krems, and decide early whether to bring a bike or hire one.
- Buy boat bike tickets at the landing; they are not sold online.
- Check DDSG's site for low-water timetable changes on the day you sail.
- Check Melk Abbey's opening hours for your dates, especially in winter.
- Look up Heurigen openings in the calendar rather than assuming a tavern is open.
- Carry a light layer even in summer; mornings on the river can be cool.

The Wachau is small enough to see in a day and good enough to deserve three. Ride one way, sail the other, leave room for a climb and a long evening over a glass of Grüner Veltliner, and if you would like someone to stitch the trains, boats and bikes together, Danube & Alps can help.
